Septuagint base · exhaustive interpretation · not a translation

Ἐν ἀρχῇ
as far as English can be made to carry it

Every load-bearing word is opened to the full range its lexicon gives it, and left open — no single English equivalent is chosen, because choosing one is where the meaning goes. Three parallel readings run against each verse: the apparatus, the unanchored text, and the anchored text. The Old Testament is read from the Greek throughout, so that when the New Testament quotes it, the quotation is visible on the page.

Conventions

Unanchored

The headword is removed entirely. The reader rebuilds the referent from the gloss-chain alone. Best for poetry, prologue, and theological argument, where the familiar word is exactly what stops the reader from seeing.

Anchored

The conventional word is kept and the chain trails behind it, set off by dashes. Best for narrative, law, and genealogy, where a reader needs to hold a referent across many verses without losing the thread.

Names

Transliterated names keep the form the Greek gives them, with the meaning in parentheses immediately after, in both reading texts. A name left unglossed is a hole in the sentence — it is a word that meant something to the first hearer and means nothing now.

Verbal aspect

Where the Greek aspect carries weight English cannot, it is stated in the text rather than footnoted: one completed act seen whole for the aorist, and kept on being for the imperfect.

The apparatus

Hebrew-to-Greek divergences stay in the notes and never enter the reading text. The Septuagint's own reading is the text; where it parts from the Masoretic Hebrew, that is a fact about the text, not a defect to be quietly repaired.

What is not added

No sense is imported that the Greek word does not carry, however familiar it is from other English versions. Where a well-known English phrase has no basis in this text, the notes say so.

Panels
Genesis 1 : 1 Septuagint · Rahlfs

Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἐποίησεν ὁ θεὸς τὸν οὐρανὸν καὶ τὴν γῆν.

The oldest manuscripts carry no spaces, no accents, no lower case. Word division is already an act of interpretation.

Notes

Lexical range, aspect, and where the Greek parts from the Hebrew

ἐν ἀρχῇ
ἀρχή holds together two things English must split: the temporal origin-point, and ruling precedence. The second sense is alive in ἄρχων, ruler, and in the ἀρχ- of every English word from monarch to architect. Neither sense is optional here.
ἐποίησεν
Aorist active indicative, 3rd singular, of ποιέω — the everyday verb for making and doing, used of potters, poets, and God alike. divergence Hebrew bārā' is a verb that in this stem never takes a human subject. ποιέω takes any subject at all. The exclusivity — this is a thing only God does — is not in the Greek.
ὁ θεός
Articular: the God, the known and definite one. Renders Hebrew Elohim, plural in form but taking singular agreement. Greek has no way to show that and does not try.
τὸν οὐρανόν
divergence Singular. Hebrew šāmayim is a dual or plural form — heavens. The LXX collapses it to one vaulted arch.
τὴν γῆν
γῆ runs from soil underfoot, to farmland, to a country, to dry land as against the sea, to the whole terrestrial realm. Pairing it with οὐρανός pulls toward the widest sense without cancelling the narrow ones.
Names
None in this verse.

Unanchored

Headwords removed — the reader rebuilds the referent

Within, inside of, and by means of the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle from which a thing proceeds, and simultaneously the headship, the rule, and the governing precedence over all that follows, He who is God definitively, the One the article marks out, the known and the only, fashioned, wrought, manufactured, produced, performed, and brought to a finished and accomplished state — one completed act seen whole, not a process observed in progress — the vaulted arch, the sky-expanse, the region above and over; and the land, the dry ground, the soil, the territory, the terrestrial realm as the counterpart-below.

Anchored

Headwords kept — the chain trails behind

In the beginning — within, inside of, and by means of the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle from which a thing proceeds, and at the same time the headship, the rule, and the governing precedence over all that follows — God, He who is God definitively, the One the article marks out, the known and the only, made: fashioned, wrought, manufactured, produced, performed, and brought to a finished and accomplished state in one completed act seen whole, not a process observed in progress — the heaven, the vaulted arch, the sky-expanse, the region above and over, and the earth, the land, the dry ground, the soil, the territory, the terrestrial realm as the counterpart-below.

Genesis 1 : 2 Septuagint · Rahlfs

ἡ δὲ γῆ ἦν ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος, καὶ σκότος ἐπάνω τῆς ἀβύσσου, καὶ πνεῦμα θεοῦ ἐπεφέρετο ἐπάνω τοῦ ὕδατος.

Closed, the line is what a fourth-century reader actually met on the page.

Notes

Lexical range, aspect, and where the Greek parts from the Hebrew

δέ
A soft adversative, not the hard ἀλλά. It pivots the narrative without contradicting what came before: however, whereas, on the other hand.
ἦν
Imperfect of εἰμί. Durative — a condition that held and kept holding, not a snapshot. English was is aspectually blank, so the duration has to be spelled out.
ἀόρατος καὶ ἀκατασκεύαστος
divergence The single largest departure in the verse, and possibly in the chapter. Hebrew tōhû wā-bōhû is desolation and emptiness — trackless waste, ruin, the wilderness where nothing is. The Greek says invisible and un-outfitted: a thing that exists but is neither perceptible nor yet furnished. On this reading Philo and much of the patristic tradition built a pre-cosmic, formless substrate. It is not what the Hebrew says.
ἀόρατος
Un-seeable, not presenting itself to sight. The same word Paul uses of God himself — the image of the invisible God, Col 1:15.
ἀκατασκεύαστος
Built on κατασκευή: the full outfitting of a house or a ship — fittings, provisions, installed equipment. Formless is too abstract; the word means not yet fitted out.
ἄβυσσος
a-βυσσος, without a sounding — a depth no plumb line reaches. Renders Hebrew tĕhôm, the primeval deep.
πνεῦμα θεοῦ
Anarthrous. πνεῦμα and Hebrew rûaḥ share the same triple range — wind, breath, spirit — one of the rare places where the two languages line up exactly and nothing at all is lost.
ἐπεφέρετο
divergence Imperfect middle-passive of ἐπιφέρω, to be borne upon. Hebrew mĕraḥepet is a bird word — hovering, fluttering, brooding over young, used of an eagle over the nest in Deut 32:11. The Greek is a current, not a wing.
ἐπάνω τοῦ ὕδατος
not in this text The familiar face of the waters is not here. The Hebrew has face of at both points; the LXX drops it at both. ὕδατος is also singular against Hebrew plural mayim.
Names
None in this verse.

Unanchored

Headwords removed — the reader rebuilds the referent

But — however, whereas, on the other hand — the land, the dry ground, the terrestrial realm was, and kept on being as a standing and continuing condition and not a momentary one, unseeable, un-viewable, not presenting itself to the eye, imperceptible to sight; and un-built-out, unfurnished, unequipped, unprovisioned, not fitted out with its appointments, lacking the constructed arrangement that a full outfitting would supply. And obscurity, gloom, the privation and the absence of light lay upon, over, and atop the bottomless, the depth without a floor to sound, the unfathomable and unmeasurable deep. And Breath, wind, moving air, respiration, Spirit — God's own, divine in character — was being borne along, carried over, swept upon, brought continuously to bear over and atop the water.

Anchored

Headwords kept — the chain trails behind

But the earth — the land, the dry ground, the terrestrial realm — was, and kept on being as a standing and continuing condition, invisible — unseeable, un-viewable, not presenting itself to the eye, imperceptible to sight — and unfurnished — un-built-out, unequipped, unprovisioned, not fitted out with its appointments, lacking the constructed arrangement that a full outfitting would supply. And darkness — obscurity, gloom, the privation and the absence of light — was upon, over, and atop the abyss — the bottomless, the depth without a floor to sound, the unfathomable and unmeasurable deep. And the Breath of God — wind, moving air, respiration, Spirit, God's own and divine in character — was being borne along — carried over, swept upon, brought continuously to bear — over and atop the water.

John 1 : 1 Nestle-Aland

Ἐν ἀρχῇ ἦν ὁ λόγος, καὶ ὁ λόγος ἦν πρὸς τὸν θεόν, καὶ θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος.

Closed, the first three letters are the same three that open Genesis. That is the point of the verse.

Notes

Lexical range, aspect, and the grammar English cannot reproduce

Ἐν ἀρχῇ
Word for word the opening of LXX Genesis. John is quoting the Greek Old Testament, not the Hebrew. On a Septuagint base the citation is simply visible on the page instead of buried in a footnote — which is the whole argument for reading the Old Testament in Greek alongside the New.
ἦν
Imperfect of εἰμί — and emphatically not ἐγένετο, came to be, which the prologue reserves for created things from verse 3 onward. The entire architecture of the passage rests on this one contrast, and English was carries none of it. Hence the expansion: was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be.
ὁ λόγος
Articular. The range: the spoken word, the utterance, the thing said; the account or reckoning, as in rendering an account; ratio and proportion; reasoned discourse; and in Stoic usage the rational principle ordering the cosmos. English Word keeps the speech and drops the reason. Latin had the same problem — verbum won, though sermo held more.
πρὸς τὸν θεόν
πρός with the accusative is directional: toward, facing, up against. It is not παρά (beside), not μετά (in company with), not σύν (together with) — any of which the writer could have used. English with is static; πρός is vectored. This is the largest single loss in the standard versions.
θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος
The predicate θεός stands without the article and is thrown to the front; ὁ λόγος carries the article and is the subject. A fronted anarthrous predicate nominative is normally qualitative — it says what the subject is by nature, not which individual it is identical with. So: not the Word was the Father, which the article placement refuses, and not a god, which the fronting refuses. Word order does work here that no English word order can do, which is why this clause has been fought over for eighteen centuries.
Names
None. λόγος is expanded rather than transliterated; Logos is retained only where the reading text needs the noun to function as a title.

Unanchored

Headwords removed — the reader rebuilds the referent

Within, inside of, and by means of the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle, the headship and the governing precedence over all that follows — the same opening with which the first book begins — there was, and was continuously, and was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be, the Utterance, the spoken Word, the thing-said, the Account, the Reckoning, the Rationale, the Reasoned Discourse, the intelligible ordering by which a mind is expressed and by which that mind itself coheres. And the Utterance, the Account, was and remained toward the God — facing Him, oriented to Him, in living motion and address toward Him, held face to face in a nearness that never collapses into sameness. And everything that God is by nature, deity in full and undiminished kind, was what that Utterance was.

Anchored

Headwords kept — the chain trails behind

In the beginning — within, inside of, and by means of the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle, the headship and the governing precedence over all that follows — was the Word, the Logos: the Utterance, the spoken Word, the thing-said, the Account, the Reckoning, the Rationale, the Reasoned Discourse, the intelligible ordering by which a mind is expressed and by which that mind itself coheres — was, and was continuously, and was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be. And the Word was with God — toward the God, facing Him, oriented to Him, in living motion and address toward Him, held face to face in a nearness that never collapses into sameness. And the Word was God — everything that God is by nature, deity in full and undiminished kind, was what that Word was.

John 1 : 2 Nestle-Aland

οὗτος ἦν ἐν ἀρχῇ πρὸς τὸν θεόν.

Six words, and every one of them has already appeared in verse 1.

Notes

Lexical range, aspect, and the grammar English cannot reproduce

οὗτος
Near demonstrative — this one here. It gathers up everything just said and pins it to one referent, which is why the expansion adds and no other.
Function of the verse
It restates 1a and 1b together, and its work is to block a reading in which the θεός of 1c is a different party from the τὸν θεόν of 1b. The one who is God by nature is the same one who stands face to face toward God. The verse looks redundant in English and is not redundant in Greek.
ἦν
The fourth imperfect in two verses. Still no ἐγένετο. The writer is being relentless, and a version that renders all four as a flat was hides the relentlessness.
Names
None in this verse.

Unanchored

Headwords removed — the reader rebuilds the referent

This same one, this very one and no other, was — continuously, and without any point of having come to be — within, inside of, and by means of the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle, facing, oriented toward, and in living address toward the God.

Anchored

Headwords kept — the chain trails behind

This one — this same one, this very one and no other — was, continuously and without any point of having come to be, in the beginning — the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle — with God: toward Him, facing Him, oriented to Him, in living address toward Him.

Convention demonstrated · John 1 : 6

None of the four verses above contains a proper name, so here is the naming rule working on the first one the prologue offers — a verse that also puts the ἦν / ἐγένετο contrast on display.

Ἐγένετο ἄνθρωπος ἀπεσταλμένος παρὰ θεοῦ, ὄνομα αὐτῷ Ἰωάννης·

The sentence opens with the verb the Logos was never given.

Notes

Lexical range, aspect, and the naming rule

Ἐγένετο
Aorist middle of γίνομαι — came to be, came into existence, arose. Placed first for emphasis. This is precisely the verb withheld from the Logos in verses 1–2, and it arrives the moment a creature enters the sentence.
ἀπεσταλμένος
Perfect passive participle of ἀποστέλλω — sent off on another's authority, dispatched with a commission. The perfect makes it a standing status, not a past errand. Root of ἀπόστολος.
Ἰωάννης
name A Greek transliteration of Hebrew Yôḥānān, a compound of the divine name with the verb ḥānan, to show favour or be gracious: Yah has shown favour. The name is kept in the Greek form and glossed in parentheses. It is not translated away, because it is what the man was called; it is not left bare, because to the first hearers it was a sentence.

Unanchored

Headwords removed — the reader rebuilds the referent

There came to be, there arose, there was brought into existence — and it is this verb, the one withheld until now, that arrives first in the sentence — a man, a human being, one sent off, dispatched, and commissioned on another's authority, from alongside and from the side of God; and the name belonging to him: Iōannēs (Ἰωάννης, from Hebrew Yôḥānān, "Yah has shown favour").

Anchored

Headwords kept — the chain trails behind

There came to be a man — there arose, there was brought into existence, a human being — sent from God: sent off, dispatched, and commissioned on another's authority, from alongside and from the side of God — whose name was John (Ἰωάννης, from Hebrew Yôḥānān, "Yah has shown favour").

Genesis follows the Septuagint (Rahlfs–Hanhart); John follows Nestle-Aland. Divergence notes compare the Greek against the Masoretic Hebrew and never alter the reading text. Where an English phrase familiar from other versions has no basis in the Greek, the notes mark it rather than supplying it.
