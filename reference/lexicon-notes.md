# Lexicon notes — recurring gloss-chains

Seeded from `reference/gold-standard.html` (GEN 1:1–2, JHN 1:1–2, JHN 1:6).

**These strings are copied verbatim into new chapters.** ἀρχή must read the same way in
Gen 1:1 and John 1:1 — that consistency is what makes the quotation visible on the page.
If a chain has to change, change it here first and re-render every chapter that uses it.

Conventions in this file:

- **anchor** — the conventional headword the anchored text keeps.
- **chain** — the gloss-chain itself. This is what the unanchored text carries alone and
  what trails behind the anchor in the anchored text.
- **short** — the reduced form used when the word is being restated rather than
  introduced. John 1:2 restates John 1:1 and takes the short forms throughout.
- Chains are given uncapitalised; capitalise at the head of a sentence as needed.

## Prepositions and particles

### ἐν

- **anchor:** in
- **chain:** `within, inside of, and by means of`
- **used:** GEN 1:1, JHN 1:1, JHN 1:2

Locative, instrumental, and sphere-of-operation senses are all live and none is chosen.
The chain is fixed and does not reduce, even in restatement.

### πρός + accusative

- **anchor:** with
- **canonical chain:** toward, facing, oriented to, in living motion and address toward
- **used:** JHN 1:1, JHN 1:2

This chain takes an object, so unlike the others it is not a fixed string: the object is
named once at the head and pronominalised through the rest of the chain. Surface forms as
established:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| JHN 1:1 unanchored | `toward the God — facing Him, oriented to Him, in living motion and address toward Him, held face to face in a nearness that never collapses into sameness` |
| JHN 1:1 anchored | `with God — toward the God, facing Him, oriented to Him, in living motion and address toward Him, held face to face in a nearness that never collapses into sameness` |
| JHN 1:2 unanchored (short) | `facing, oriented toward, and in living address toward the God` |
| JHN 1:2 anchored (short) | `with God: toward Him, facing Him, oriented to Him, in living address toward Him` |

The extension `held face to face in a nearness that never collapses into sameness` belongs to
the full form only; the short form drops it.

Directional, never static. Note in the apparatus wherever παρά / μετά / σύν were available
and not used. English *with* is the largest single loss in the standard versions, so the
anchored text keeps *with* only as the anchor and immediately corrects it.

### ἐπάνω

- **anchor:** upon / over
- **chain:** `upon, over, and atop`
- **short:** `over and atop`
- **used:** GEN 1:2 (twice — full at the abyss, short at the water)

### παρά + genitive

- **anchor:** from
- **chain:** `from alongside and from the side of`
- **used:** JHN 1:6

### δέ

- **anchor:** but
- **chain:** `however, whereas, on the other hand`
- **used:** GEN 1:2

Soft adversative. Never rendered with the force of ἀλλά.

## Divine designations

### ὁ θεός — articular

- **anchor:** God
- **chain:** `He who is God definitively, the One the article marks out, the known and the only`
- **used:** GEN 1:1

### θεός — anarthrous, qualitative predicate

- **anchor:** God
- **chain:** `everything that God is by nature, deity in full and undiminished kind`
- **used:** JHN 1:1 (θεὸς ἦν ὁ λόγος)

Fronted anarthrous predicate nominative. The chain must say what the subject is *by nature*
without asserting identity of person and without indefiniteness. Flag the fronting in the
notes every time it occurs.

### θεοῦ — anarthrous genitive

- **anchor:** of God
- **chain:** `God's own, divine in character`
- **anchored form:** `God's own and divine in character`
- **used:** GEN 1:2 (πνεῦμα θεοῦ)

### τὸν θεόν — accusative under πρός

- **anchor:** God
- **rendering:** `the God` — the article is carried into English here, against normal English
  usage, because John 1:1c turns on the contrast between articular τὸν θεόν and anarthrous θεός.
- **used:** JHN 1:1, JHN 1:2

## Verbs

### εἰμί — imperfect ἦν

Two settled forms. They are not interchangeable.

- **state of a creature** (GEN 1:2): `was, and kept on being as a standing and continuing condition`
  - unanchored extends: `was, and kept on being as a standing and continuing condition and not a momentary one`
- **pre-existence, against ἐγένετο** (JHN 1:1, JHN 1:2): `was, and was continuously, and was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be`
  - unanchored at JHN 1:1 opens: `there was, and was continuously, and was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be`
  - **short** (JHN 1:2): `continuously, and without any point of having come to be`

The second form is required anywhere the ἦν / ἐγένετο contrast is in play. Never flatten
either to *was*.

### γίνομαι — aorist ἐγένετο

- **anchor:** there came to be
- **chain:** `there came to be, there arose, there was brought into existence`
- **used:** JHN 1:6

The verb withheld from the Logos in JHN 1:1–2. Wherever it stands against ἦν in the same
context, say so in the notes.

### ποιέω — aorist

- **anchor:** made
- **chain:** `fashioned, wrought, manufactured, produced, performed, and brought to a finished and accomplished state`
- **used:** GEN 1:1

Carries the aorist formula immediately after it (below).

### ἐπιφέρω — imperfect middle-passive

- **anchor:** was being borne along
- **chain:** `carried over, swept upon, brought continuously to bear`
- **used:** GEN 1:2

### ἀποστέλλω — perfect passive participle

- **anchor:** sent
- **chain:** `sent off, dispatched, and commissioned on another's authority`
- **unanchored form:** `one sent off, dispatched, and commissioned on another's authority`
- **used:** JHN 1:6

Perfect — a standing status, not a past errand.

## Nouns

### ἀρχή

- **anchor:** in the beginning (with ἐν)
- **chain (full, GEN 1:1):** `the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle from which a thing proceeds, and simultaneously the headship, the rule, and the governing precedence over all that follows`
  - anchored at GEN 1:1 reads `and at the same time` where the unanchored reads `and simultaneously`
- **chain (condensed, JHN 1:1):** `the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle, the headship and the governing precedence over all that follows`
- **short (JHN 1:2):** `the first moment, the origin-point, the source, the first principle`
- **used:** GEN 1:1, JHN 1:1, JHN 1:2

Both halves — temporal origin and ruling precedence — are obligatory in the full and
condensed forms. The short form drops the precedence half only because JHN 1:2 is restating
JHN 1:1, where it has already been given.

At JHN 1:1 the unanchored text adds the pointer `— the same opening with which the first
book begins —` inside the chain. Use that device only where the Greek is quoting the LXX
word for word.

### λόγος

- **anchor:** the Word, the Logos
- **chain:** `the Utterance, the spoken Word, the thing-said, the Account, the Reckoning, the Rationale, the Reasoned Discourse, the intelligible ordering by which a mind is expressed and by which that mind itself coheres`
- **short:** `the Utterance, the Account`
- **used:** JHN 1:1

Capitalised throughout — the chain is functioning as a title. *Logos* is transliterated in
the anchored text only, and only because the reading text needs the noun to work as a title;
it is not treated as a proper name and takes no name gloss.

### οὐρανός

- **anchor:** the heaven
- **chain:** `the vaulted arch, the sky-expanse, the region above and over`
- **used:** GEN 1:1

Singular in the LXX against Hebrew dual/plural *šāmayim*. Flag `divergence`.

### γῆ

- **anchor:** the earth
- **chain (full, GEN 1:1):** `the land, the dry ground, the soil, the territory, the terrestrial realm as the counterpart-below`
- **short (GEN 1:2):** `the land, the dry ground, the terrestrial realm`
- **used:** GEN 1:1, GEN 1:2

`as the counterpart-below` is used only where γῆ is paired with οὐρανός.

### σκότος

- **anchor:** darkness
- **chain:** `obscurity, gloom, the privation and the absence of light`
- **used:** GEN 1:2

### ἄβυσσος

- **anchor:** the abyss
- **chain:** `the bottomless, the depth without a floor to sound, the unfathomable and unmeasurable deep`
- **used:** GEN 1:2

### πνεῦμα

- **anchor:** the Breath
- **chain:** `wind, moving air, respiration, Spirit`
- **unanchored form:** `Breath, wind, moving air, respiration, Spirit`
- **used:** GEN 1:2

The one place where Greek and Hebrew line up exactly — πνεῦμα and *rûaḥ* share the same
triple range and nothing is lost. Say so in the notes rather than choosing.

### ὕδωρ

- **anchor:** the water
- **used:** GEN 1:2

No chain — the word is not doing lexical work here. Singular against Hebrew plural *mayim*;
flag `divergence`. **The Hebrew's *face of* is absent from the LXX at both points in this
verse and must not appear in either reading text.**

### ἄνθρωπος

- **anchor:** a man
- **chain:** `a human being`
- **used:** JHN 1:6

### οὗτος

- **anchor:** this one
- **chain:** `this same one, this very one and no other`
- **used:** JHN 1:2

`and no other` carries the near demonstrative's work of pinning the referent.

### Adjectives at GEN 1:2

- **ἀόρατος** — anchor `invisible`; chain `unseeable, un-viewable, not presenting itself to
  the eye, imperceptible to sight`
- **ἀκατασκεύαστος** — anchor `unfurnished`; chain `un-built-out, unequipped, unprovisioned,
  not fitted out with its appointments, lacking the constructed arrangement that a full
  outfitting would supply`
  - the unanchored text carries `unfurnished` inside the chain, immediately after
    `un-built-out`; the anchored text lifts it out to serve as the anchor

Both stand under one `divergence` flag against Hebrew *tōhû wā-bōhû*. *Formless* is too
abstract for ἀκατασκεύαστος and is not used.

## Aspect formulas

Fixed wording. These go in the reading text, never in a footnote.

| aspect | formula |
| --- | --- |
| aorist | `one completed act seen whole, not a process observed in progress` |
| imperfect (state) | `and kept on being as a standing and continuing condition` |
| imperfect (εἰμί, pre-existence) | `was already in unbroken existence without any point of having come to be` |
| perfect | a standing resultant state, not a past event — worded to the verb |

At GEN 1:1 the aorist formula is set off by em dashes in the unanchored text and folded in
with `in` in the anchored text (`...accomplished state in one completed act seen whole...`).

## Names

Gloss on first occurrence per chapter, bare thereafter.

| Greek | unanchored form | anchored form | gloss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ἰωάννης | Iōannēs | John | `(Ἰωάννης, from Hebrew Yôḥānān, "Yah has shown favour")` |

Rendered markup is identical in both panels apart from the form:
`<span class="name">FORM</span> <span class="paren">GLOSS</span>`. In the JSON this is
written `[[name:Ἰωάννης]]` and the form is selected by panel.

## Notes for `build/validate.py`

Two things the seeded chains show, which a naive check will get wrong:

1. **Anchor words legitimately appear inside unanchored chains.** `unfurnished` is the
   anchor for ἀκατασκεύαστος and also sits inside its chain; `Word` is the anchor for λόγος
   and also appears in `the spoken Word`; `God` is the anchor for θεός and appears in
   `He who is God definitively`. The rule "unanchored contains no headword from the anchor
   list" has to mean *no bare headword standing where the chain should be* — a plain
   substring test fails on all three.

2. **Banned imported phrases.** Seed the list with `face of the waters` and `face of the
   deep` (GEN 1:2 — the LXX drops *face of* at both points).
