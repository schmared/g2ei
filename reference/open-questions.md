# Open questions

Judgment calls made during a multi-chapter run, for an edit pass afterwards. Each one records the
reading the text has now (the nearest precedent), the alternative, and examples side by side. None
of these is a defect under a rule already set — those are fixed when found. Settle one, apply it
across the corpus, and delete its entry.

## Q36. Two translations joined with *and*

**Where:** a rough scan finds about 440 units over 57 words. The largest groups: ἀντί's `set against and compared
with` (45 units, from GEN 2:21); οἰκία's `the household and inmates of the house` (43, GEN 17:12); ἵστημι's `set
up and raise` (28, GEN 6:18); μέρος's `the part one takes in and the part assigned` (22); γένος's `a tribe and
caste` and `sex and gender` (18 each); ἐκβάλλω's `cast out and banished` (17); παροικέω's `live in and sojourn`
(17).

**Now:** one item holds two of the card's translations, joined with an *and* the entry does not print. In ἀντί the
entry has «set against» the other, «compared with» it. In οἰκία it has «the household», i. e. «inmates of the
house». In ἵστημι it has «to set up» the loom, or «to raise» the mast. These are the first drafts' joins, from
before Q2 set the delimiter. Q2 caught pieces of one translation joined that way, not two translations. The
check reads runs, and a run's pieces are inside one translation. μένω's `remain as one was and hold good and remain
contented` was one of them, and has been fixed: its card gives the three as senses of their own, and the chain
carried *hold good* and *remain contented* again after it.

- now: `set against and compared with` (ἀντί)
- one item, the delimiter: `set against / compared with`
- two items: `set against, compared with`

**The difference:** *and* is a word the Greek does not have, which CLAUDE.md rules out as the delimiter. The two
alternatives part on whether two translations in one numbered sense are one sense or two. A comma reads them as
two senses, which is how the card lists them. The delimiter reads them as one, which is how the entry prints them,
inside one numbered sense, and with *or* in ἵστημι's case.

**Recommendation:** two items, since each `<tr>` is one translation. Where the entry joins the two with *or*, as
ἵστημι's does across its example, the *or* rule gives one item with the delimiter. Then add a check: no item may
join with *and* two translations the card gives separately.
